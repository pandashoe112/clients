Section 01

How often your website appeared in Google, and how often people clicked

These four numbers describe the whole website across every search term. Impressions count how many times a page of yours was shown in Google's results. Clicks count how many of those turned into a visit. Click-through rate is simply clicks divided by impressions, so it measures how appealing your listing looks once someone sees it.

Both weeks are measured the same way, one week against the week immediately before it, so seasonality and day of week are not distorting the comparison.

Why the last figure matters more than the other three

Every click the old website earned in its final week came from someone typing your business name into Google. Those people already knew about you and would have found you anyway. In the first week on the new website, two clicks came from people searching for a service instead: electrician williamstown and electrician near me. That is the first evidence the website can win a customer who has never heard of Revelectrical, which is the entire point of the rebuild.

Section 03

Searches where your website moved up Google's rankings

Average position is where your listing sat in Google's results, counted from the top. Lower is better, so a move from 48.9 to 37.9 means you climbed eleven places. Realistically, clicks only start arriving in volume once a term reaches the first page, which is roughly position 10.

Section 05

Why the average ranking position went down while everything else went up

Across the whole website, average position moved from 44.7 to 49.8. Read on its own that looks like a fall, and it is worth explaining before it appears in any future report.

Average position is an average across every search term the website appears for. In the week just gone, several hundred appearances arrived on brand new terms that currently sit between position 70 and 90, because those pages have only just been published and Google has not yet decided where they belong. Adding a large number of low positions to the calculation pulls the average down, even though nothing that was already ranking has moved backwards.

Section 06

What we expect to happen over the next few months

One week of data shows direction, not results. Here is the sequence we would expect from a launch that has started this way, and what we will be reporting against each time.

  1. Weeks 2 to 4

    New pages settle into a real position

    The switchboard, solar battery and EV charging pages should climb out of the 70 to 90 range as Google works out what they are about. The website-wide average position will keep looking soft while this happens.

  2. Weeks 4 to 8

    Suburb pages start competing on the first two pages

    Newport, Williamstown, Essendon and Airport West are the terms with the most existing demand. Newport alone was shown 104 times in one week at position 34.5, so moving it towards the first page is the largest single opportunity on the website.

  3. Weeks 8 to 12

    Enquiries, not appearances, become the measure

    Once terms reach the first page, clicks follow, and the reporting shifts from how often you were seen to how many quote requests came through the website form.

Section 07

How this data was gathered and where to treat it carefully

Figures for 10 to 16 August come from Google Search Console for revelectrical.com.au. Figures for 3 to 9 August come from the Top Search Queries on Google report inside the previous Wix dashboard, which draws on the same Google data.

The Wix export lists a number of search terms more than once and its visible rows do not add up to its own summary total, so the term level figures from the old website should be read as indicative. The summary totals for that week, 1,211 appearances and 4 clicks, are reliable, and every percentage in Section 01 is calculated from those. Where a search term is described as new in Section 04, it means it did not appear in last week's report rather than that it was definitively at zero.

Read the click numbers with care

Click volumes at this stage are small in absolute terms. Ten clicks is ten clicks, and percentages will swing hard week to week until the numbers grow. The reliable signals in this report are the increase in appearances, the arrival of non-brand clicks, and the appearance of three service categories the old website was invisible for.
